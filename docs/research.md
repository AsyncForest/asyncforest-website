## PART 2 — MARKET RESEARCH: NICHES & PROPOSITIONS

### 2.1 Independent technical assurance & technical due diligence (TDD)

**The market is real, active, and buys exactly what Franks proposes to sell.** Per Software Development UK, "In the UK, TDD costs typically range from £20,000 to £150,000, usually less than 1% of total deal value" (a mid-sized ~£6m acquisition incurs ~£40,000 in TDD costs), commissioned mainly by the **buyer/investor**. Fees are structured as **fixed-price for well-defined scopes or time-and-materials for complex/evolving reviews** — directly validating Franks's "capped T&M on unfamiliar codebases, itemised SOW" instinct.

**Buyers:** VC and PE firms (Series A through growth/buyout), corporate acquirers, and founders doing "sell-side"/readiness prep. TDD is "an increasingly important aspect of the private equity investment process."

**Existing players / comparables:**
- **Boutiques & specialists:** Crosslake (proprietary data from 4,000+ tech M&A deals), Code & Co. (375+ deals, >$50bn), Diligize, Palladium, Seedcloud, JMAN, OMMAX — plus UK-focused **Boxsail** and **TechDD (techdd.co.uk)**, which explicitly productise "investor-grade review of code and architecture," red/amber/green risk matrices, 2-page IC summaries, and 5–10 working-day turnarounds.
- **Solo/senior-practitioner model is credible:** Boxsail markets "senior practitioner assessment" combined with AI-powered codebase analysis; Gradion sells a fixed-price (€6,000, 1–2 week) senior-architect-led TDD explicitly undercutting "boutique firms at €30K–80K." Independent consultants charge **30–50% less than boutique firms and 60–80% less than the Big Four** for equivalent talent.

**Verdict:** A solo operator *can* credibly compete, **specifically because** deliverable clarity and senior-practitioner judgement are what buyers value, and because VCs increasingly want fast (days not weeks), architecture-and-AI-literate reviews. Franks's edge: most TDD boutiques are strong on generic code quality/scalability but **weak on AI-specific risk** (model/data governance, LLM concurrency/cost/resilience, responsible-AI exposure) — his sweet spot.

### 2.2 AI assurance, AI audit & responsible-AI advisory (UK)

**This is a government-tailwind market and his academic credibility is a genuine differentiator.** Per DSIT's report *Assuring a Responsible Future for AI* (published 6 Nov 2024 by the Responsible Technology Adoption Unit), the UK AI assurance market comprises **"524 firms supplying AI assurance goods and services…generating an estimated £1.01bn and employ[ing] an estimated 12,572 employees,"** with **"the potential to exceed £6.53bn by 2035 if opportunities to drive future growth are realised."** The UK has "built the most structured national approach to growing an AI assurance market," with DSIT's *Trusted third-party AI assurance roadmap*, an AI Assurance Platform, the AI Standards Hub (Alan Turing Institute/BSI/NPL), and ISO/IEC 42001 emerging as the anchor standard.

**Demand signals:** PwC UK launched a formal "Assurance for AI" service; Deloitte markets a "Trustworthy AI" framework; the DRCF (UK regulators) has published landscape reviews. The recurring buyer pain, per PwC UK's Audit CTO Marc Bena: "Companies are developing proofs of concept … but are hesitant about 'pressing go' and moving to production. Independent assurance can give organisations the additional confidence they need to take that next step."

**Competitor landscape:** Big Four (PwC, Deloitte) at the top; AI-assurance platforms (Holistic AI, Credo AI, Monitaur); certification bodies (BSI, DNV) for ISO 42001. **Note a structural constraint:** UKAS accreditation bodies cannot review "assurance as a service" providers, and there is **no chartered professional body for AI assurance yet** — so the market rewards *demonstrable expert credibility* (exactly what a Warwick PhD + KCL/Notre Dame responsible-AI teaching + production AI track record supplies).

**Verdict:** High-growth, credibility-driven, and his responsible-AI positioning is a **real moat** here — but caution: much of the "AI governance" market is heavy on policy/framework/compliance work (potentially admin-heavy, context-switching, committee sales cycles) that may fit his neurodivergent working preferences *less* well than crisp technical audits. Recommend he take the **technical** slice of AI assurance (model/data/architecture verification, AI-in-production resilience) rather than the governance-policy slice.

### 2.3 The "vibe-coded apps / AI-generated code / shadow-AI" enterprise thesis

**Steel-man (the thesis is strongly supported by evidence):**

### 2.4 Security-audit / pen-test-adjacent assurance & UK certifications

- **CREST is the recognised UK standard** for penetration testing and is **effectively mandatory for public-sector, financial-services, and regulated procurement** (CBEST, G-Cloud, CHECK draw from the CREST-qualified pool). Individual certs that carry weight: **OSCP, CREST CRT/CCT, GXPN**.
- **But CREST is a company + individual accreditation aimed at formal pen-testing.** For a solo consultant, pursuing full CREST accreditation is heavy and arguably off-strategy. **Recommendation: do NOT position as a CREST pen-testing provider.** Instead, position as **"security-informed architecture & code assurance / security review"** — clearly scoped as expert technical review, explicitly *not* a CREST-accredited penetration test — and **partner/refer** to a CREST firm when a formal pen test is required. Cyber Essentials (org-level baseline) is cheap and worth holding for his own Ltd company credibility, and useful context knowledge to advise startups.
- This keeps him out of the commoditised, certification-gated pen-test market while letting him sell the higher-judgement layer (threat modelling, secure architecture review, AI/LLM security review) where his seniority commands a premium.

### 2.5 Fractional CTO / technical advisory / architecture review (UK)

- **Rates:** UK fractional CTO day rates cluster **£800–£2,000/day**; London median contractor CTO rate reported at **£1,350/day** (IT Jobs Watch, Feb 2026); monthly retainers **£2,000–£10,000** depending on days/week. Specialist premiums (fintech, AI, healthcare compliance) push the top end.
- **Structure:** typically 1–3 days/week, 6–18 month engagements; often network/referral-sourced; platforms exist (Fractional Quest, A.Team, GoFractional).
- **Fit caveat:** true fractional-CTO work is **embedded, ongoing, people-management-heavy and context-switching-intensive** — a *poor* fit for a deep-work, low-admin, low-context-switch operator. **Recommendation: offer time-boxed "architecture review" and "technical advisory sprints" as productised engagements** (see 2.6) rather than open-ended fractional-CTO retainers. His £700 (band £650–850) day rate is *below* the fractional-CTO market — signalling room to raise, and a reason to price by *packaged outcome* rather than by day.

### 2.6 Productisation strategy (the core recommendation for his working style)

The independent-consulting literature converges hard on one model that fits a neurodivergent, deep-work solo operator almost perfectly: **productise the diagnostic entry point, then let it lead to scoped follow-on work.**

- **The trap to escape:** hourly/day-rate billing caps income and *penalises* getting faster (you earn less as you improve). Productised consultants earn an effective **$300–$600/hour vs $150–$300 for custom billing**.
- **The gateway-diagnostic pattern (repeatedly evidenced):** Mike Gammarino leads with a **$2,500 sales-funnel audit** (fixed scope, fixed price, clear deliverable) — most clients then move into **$25,000+** engagements. "They productize the entry point — a fixed-scope diagnostic that leads naturally into custom work." This works *best* "for solo consultants and small firms where the founder is still involved in sales and delivery."
- **Productised pricing norms:** diagnostic audits **$2,000–$5,000**; strategy sprints **$5,000–$15,000**; implementation plans **$8,000–$25,000**; advisory subscriptions **$3,500–$4,000/month**. Fixed, transparent, tiered (usually 2–3 tiers by a scope variable like company size/codebase size).
- **Outcome, not activity:** "Instead of 'IT consulting' → 'Security audit that delivers a prioritised action plan for protecting your data.'" This is *exactly* Franks's stated deliverable: "point-in-time, scope-limited written reports with prioritised, actionable fixes."
- **Why it fits AuDHD working preferences:** repeatable process, templated delivery, defined scope kills scope-creep, no unlimited-time commitment, minimal meetings ("no meetings required" is literally a selling point competitors use), predictable cash flow, and each engagement is a self-contained deep-work block rather than constant context-switching.

## Recommendations

**Lead with two primary niches, hold one as an emerging bet:**

1. **PRIMARY — Independent technical + AI due diligence for VCs/PE and pre-raise startups.** Mature buyers who already pay £20k–£150k, value senior-practitioner judgement, want speed, and increasingly need *AI-specific* diligence that generic TDD boutiques do poorly. Deliverable = his exact proposition (scoped report, RAG matrix, prioritised fixes). Lowest-churn pipeline (repeat VC relationships). **Start here.**

2. **PRIMARY — Independent AI-in-production / responsible-AI *technical* assurance for startups & scaleups.** Government tailwind (DSIT-backed ~£1bn market projected toward £6.53bn by 2035), credibility-driven, where his PhD + KCL/Notre Dame teaching + production AI track record are a real moat. Take the *technical* verification slice (model/data/architecture, LLM concurrency/resilience/cost), not the policy-committee governance slice.

3. **EMERGING BET — Vibe-code / AI-generated app hardening audits**, delivered *through* the two channels above (startup pre-production hardening; DD on AI-built targets). The evidence base is overwhelming and analyst-validated, the UK solo/boutique niche is near-empty whitespace, and the productised model is already proven at $1.5k–$30k. **Do not** build the business around an enterprise-CISO "assure employee-built apps" sales motion yet — that buyer is still forming and the cycle suits his style poorly. Revisit escalating into enterprise as the market matures.

**Business-model recommendations:**
- **Productise everything** into 3–5 fixed-scope, publicly-priced assessment products (see menu). This is the single highest-leverage decision for a low-admin, deep-work, neurodivergent operator.
- **Use the gateway-diagnostic pattern:** a sharp, affordable fixed-scope audit as the front door that naturally leads to scoped follow-on work.
- **Migrate off pure day-rate** toward packaged/value pricing; reserve capped T&M only for unfamiliar codebases. Raise effective rates above the current £700/day.
- **Do NOT pursue CREST accreditation or position as a pen-test provider;** sell security-informed architecture/code review and refer formal pen tests to CREST partners. Hold Cyber Essentials for baseline credibility.
- **Do NOT default to embedded fractional-CTO retainers;** offer time-boxed architecture advisory sprints instead.
- **Every SOW:** point-in-time, scope-limited, limitation-of-liability, "technical observations not legal advice," itemised.

**Benchmarks that would change the strategy:**
- If **3+ VC/PE firms** become repeat TDD clients within 6–9 months → double down on DD as the flagship; consider raising rates and adding a lightweight associate for overflow.
- If inbound for **vibe-code/AI-app hardening** exceeds DD inbound → accelerate that product and consider a UK-first "AI-built app assurance" brand while the whitespace lasts.
- If **enterprise/CISO inbound** appears (employee-built-app assurance) with real budget → that signals the enterprise buyer has formed; only then invest in the longer enterprise sales motion, ideally via partnership rather than solo.
- If day-rate/T&M creeps back above ~40% of revenue → productisation is slipping; re-tighten scopes.

### 6. UK Ltd-company naming — patterns & practical guidance

**The four archetypes observed:**
- **(a) Eponymous / founder-name:** e.g., trading via a holding co (ZP Ventures → "TechDD"); "[Surname] Consulting/Advisory." *Pros:* maximal personal accountability (perfect for assurance), SEO-clean, trades on your reputation, honest about being solo. *Cons:* less scalable/saleable, can feel small.
- **(b) Descriptive / functional:** TechDD, VibeCheck, Code & Co. *Pros:* instant clarity, SEO keyword match. *Cons:* generic, hard to trademark, collision risk (see the four different "VibeCheck" products).
- **(c) Abstract / evocative:** Boxsail, Gradion, Crosslake, Palladium. *Pros:* scalable, trademarkable, hides solo status, premium feel. *Cons:* needs brand-building, no inherent meaning/SEO.
- **(d) Metaphor / concept:** Sherlock Forensics (detective = investigation/trust). *Pros:* memorable, story-rich. *Cons:* can be gimmicky; verify trademark clashes.

**UK-specific rules (Companies Act 2006 + ECCTA 2023):**
- Name must be unique; "same as"/"too like" existing names are rejected or challengeable (minor variants like adding "UK" don't count as different).
- **Sensitive words** (150+, e.g., "Group," "Trust," "Institute," "Association," and anything implying royal/government/public-authority or foreign-government connection) require Secretary-of-State or relevant-body approval.
- **ECCTA 2023 additions:** expanded sensitive-word list; names implying false connection to UK/foreign government or international organisation restricted; **names containing computer code are banned** — per GOV.UK's ECCTA 2023 company-names factsheet, *"the Registrar will have to reject names which comprise or contain a computer code,"* with non-compliance risking the name being replaced by the company number and *"fines of up to £1,000 for continued use of the banned name."* The Registrar can also strike off misleading names, and the "significant start-up cost" defence to a name challenge has been removed.
- **"Ltd/Limited" is mandatory** in the registered name; "& Co," "Associates," "Partners," "Group" carry firm-scale psychology but "Group"/"Partners" can mislead if you're solo (and "Group" is sensitive).
- **Company name ≠ trademark:** search the UK IPO register for your sector; Companies House acceptance doesn't protect you or guarantee non-infringement.
- **Domain + social handle availability** should be checked in parallel; Companies House treats "brand.co.uk Ltd" as the same as "Brand Ltd."

**Practical recommendation for you:** a **short abstract/evocative mark (archetype c) as the Ltd name, paired with a descriptive tagline** ("Independent technical assurance"), OR an **eponymous holding company + a descriptive trading name** (the TechDD/ZP Ventures model). The abstract mark gives you trademark protection, premium feel and future scalability while your *personal name and face* on the About page and LinkedIn carry the accountability. Avoid "Group"/"Partners"/"Associates" as a solo operator — they read as either misleading or, under ECCTA, sensitive.
